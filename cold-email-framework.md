# Cold Email Framework

## Purpose

The goal of a cold email is not to sell.

The goal is to earn a response.

Most cold emails fail because they focus on the sender instead of the recipient.

---

## The Framework

Pain → Impact → Insight → Question

---

## Step 1: Pain

Identify a challenge the prospect is likely experiencing.

Examples:

- Pipeline leakage
- Low response rates
- CRM inefficiencies
- Forecasting challenges
- Slow sales cycles

---

## Step 2: Impact

Connect the problem to a business outcome.

Examples:

- Lost revenue
- Missed targets
- Higher acquisition costs
- Lower productivity

---

## Step 3: Insight

Offer a perspective.

Examples:

- Market observation
- Industry trend
- Process improvement
- Revenue opportunity

---

## Step 4: Question

End with a low-friction question.

Examples:

- How are you handling this today?
- Is this something your team is focused on?
- Curious if you've seen similar challenges?

---

## Example Email

Subject: Pipeline Visibility

Hi {{First Name}},

Many growing B2B teams struggle with pipeline visibility as they scale.

The challenge usually isn't lead volume.

It's identifying where opportunities leak before forecasts are impacted.

Curious — how is your team currently tracking pipeline health?

Best,
Shashank

---

## Email Rules

Keep emails:

- Short
- Relevant
- Specific
- Easy to reply to

---

## Avoid

- Long introductions
- Feature lists
- Generic templates
- Multiple CTAs

---

## Success Metrics

Track:

- Open Rate
- Reply Rate
- Positive Reply Rate
- Meeting Rate
- Pipeline Created

---

## Key Principle

A cold email succeeds when it starts a conversation.

Not when it explains everything.
